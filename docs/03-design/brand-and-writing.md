# Brand

MaxBid  |  maxbid.com.au

Source: MaxBid Project Workbook, tab 32 Brand.

## Positioning

**Positioning statement.** For Australian resellers who buy at auction, MaxBid tells you what a lot is really worth and the most you should pay, with every number backed by evidence you can check.

**Category.** Auction bid intelligence.

**Core promise.** Know the most you should pay before the hammer falls.

**Proof points.** Evidence on every figure. Full acquisition costs including premium, GST and transport. Conservative by default. Public method and accuracy.

**Enemy.** Bidding on gut feel, and research that takes longer than the auction.

## Personality: Sharp insider

**Confident.** Knows how auctions work and explains it clearly. States what the evidence shows and what it does not.

**Warm and direct.** Talks to the reader as a knowledgeable friend would. Uses you and we.

**Plain speaking.** Complete sentences in everyday words. Every figure and term is explained. Australian English.

**On the buyer's side.** Protects the user's profit, including from the excitement of a live auction.

**Wit.** Allowed only in taglines and social posts, and only when the meaning is still obvious. Never in explanations, instructions, figures, warnings or disclaimers.

## Writing rules

| ID | Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WR01 | Write complete sentences | Every sentence in body copy has a subject and a verb. No sentence fragments such as Every cost. Every credit. One number. |
| WR02 | Use everyday words | Choose the word a customer would use. Say counts more towards the estimate rather than weighs the most. |
| WR03 | Explain terms the first time | Define target bid, limit bid, break even bid, stop line, comparable, buyer's premium, triage and credit where they first appear on a page. |
| WR04 | Say who does what | Use active voice. We reduced the price by 10 percent, rather than Price adjusted. |
| WR05 | Keep the reason | One idea per sentence, but keep the linking words such as because, so and which that explain why. |
| WR06 | No slang or metaphors in explanations | Avoid phrases such as knock out, receipts, your number or marks. Say what the thing actually is. |
| WR07 | Tell people what they can do | Instructions say the action and the result. Select any square to see where the price came from. |
| WR08 | Headings make sense alone | Short headings are fine, but every heading is followed by a plain sentence that explains the section. |
| WR09 | Name every figure | Always say what a number is. An estimated profit of $4,000, rather than $4,000 on its own. |
| WR10 | Reading level | Aim for a reading age of about 12 to 14 years. Check key pages with a readability tool before publishing. |
| WR11 | Data labels | Short uppercase labels such as TARGET BID are allowed only as labels on figures, never as sentences. |
| WR12 | House rules | Australian English. No dashes of any kind. No keynote style contrasting sentences. |

## Logo direction

**Wordmark.** MaxBid in Barlow bold. Always written MaxBid: capital M, capital B, one word. A marker yellow stop line sits beneath Bid.

**Mark.** The stop line: a heavy horizontal bar in marker yellow, taken from the bid limit marker. It doubles as the active tab marker and loading motif.

**Favicon and app icon.** Marker yellow stop line on a navy square with a thin cyan frame.

**Consistency.** One name (MaxBid), one domain (maxbid.com.au), one description used everywhere: site, social, directories and schema. Pair the name with Australia in bios and early content so search engines and AI models separate MaxBid from overseas businesses with similar names.
